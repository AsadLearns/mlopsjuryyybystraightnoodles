1. Executive Summary & Objective

Deploying a model to production is only the beginning of the MLOps lifecycle. Over time, models degrade silently due to real-world structural changes. This system provides automated continuous monitoring for a production House Price Prediction Model:

  1. Detects Data Drift: Performs continuous hypothesis testing across numerical features (area, bedrooms) using Two-Sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) tests and Population Stability Index (PSI), and categorical features (location) using Chi-Square contingency testing.
  2. Detects Concept Drift: Monitors model regression error metrics (RMSE, MAE, $R^2$) over consecutive operational time windows to detect when real-world pricing dynamics decouple from historical training weights.
  3. Autonomous Reporting: Synthesizes statistical diagnostics into a high-resolution 6-panel visualization dashboard and structured JSON scorecard.

2. Statistical & Mathematical Foundations

2.1 Data Drift (Covariate Shift)

  • Two-Sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) Test: $$D_{n, m} = \sup_x |F_{\text{reference}}(x) - F_{\text{production}}(x)|$$ Non-parametric test comparing the cumulative distribution functions. Rejects the null hypothesis (drift present) when $p\text{-value} < 0.05$.
  • Population Stability Index (PSI): $$\text{PSI} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} (A_i - E_i) \times \ln \left( \frac{A_i}{E_i} \right)$$ Quantifies divergence across $k$ population bins. $\text{PSI} < 0.1$ indicates stability, $0.1 \le \text{PSI} < 0.25$ indicates moderate drift, and $\text{PSI} \ge 0.25$ indicates severe drift.
  • Chi-Square Goodness of Fit (Categorical): $$\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$$ Measures category proportion divergence across listing locations.

2.2 Concept Drift (Performance Decay)

  • Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): Sensitive to large outlier valuation errors.
  • Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$): Measures the proportion of variance in housing prices explained by the model. Values falling below $0.60$ trigger warning alerts; negative values confirm model obsolescence.

6. Comprehensive Jury Viva Defense Q&A

Q1: What is the fundamental difference between Data Drift and Concept Drift?
Answer: Data Drift (covariate shift) refers to changes in the input feature distribution $P(X)$ without changes to the underlying target relationship $P(Y \mid X)$ (e.g., buyers viewing larger homes, but price per sqft remains unchanged). Concept Drift refers to changes in the mathematical relationship $P(Y \mid X)$ itself (e.g., identical homes doubling in price due to inflation or interest rates, making historical model weights inaccurate).
Q2: Why is the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test superior to comparing simple mean and standard deviation?
Answer: Comparing mean and standard deviation only captures first- and second-order moments and is blind to bimodal shifts, skewness, or tail anomalies. The Two-Sample KS-test compares empirical cumulative distribution functions non-parametrically across the entire distribution spectrum, making it distribution-free and highly sensitive to arbitrary shifts.
Q3: How do you interpret Population Stability Index (PSI) values?
Answer: PSI quantifies population divergence using symmetric relative entropy: $\text{PSI} < 0.1$ indicates no change; $0.1 \le \text{PSI} < 0.25$ indicates moderate shift requiring observation; and $\text{PSI} \ge 0.25$ signifies severe drift demanding immediate retraining. In our production monitoring, `area` reached $\text{PSI} = 1.56$, confirming massive distribution shift.
Q4: Why does Month 4 show a negative R² score?
Answer: An $R^2$ of $1.0$ indicates perfect predictions, while $0.0$ indicates the model performs no better than predicting the dataset mean. A negative $R^2$ (here $-4.67$) indicates that the model's residual sum of squares is substantially worse than a simple horizontal line predicting the mean house price, confirming total model failure caused by concept drift.
